The Book Not in vain
En Ru

Question everything

Villagers once came to the Buddha with a complaint. Every teacher passing through swore his teaching was the truth and the rest were frauds. So who should they believe? The Buddha said: don’t believe anything, no matter where you read it or who said it, even if I said it, unless it agrees with your own reason and your own common sense. Don’t believe something just because it’s tradition, or because it’s in a book, or because someone smart or famous said so. Test it the way you test food. Does it taste good? Is it good for you? Does it make you freer, or more dependent?

The menu is not the meal. You can compare teachings forever and never taste one. You test it on yourself. It’s like learning to swim: sooner or later you have to get in the water.

A spoon does not taste the flavor of the soup.
Buddha

There’s one mountain, and many trails lead to the top. You can climb it different ways, or never make it up at all. If you see a ray of light, walk toward it. Maybe it makes no sense. But sitting in the dark makes no sense either way. Think, try, watch what works.

Same with the joyful path. Don’t take what I say on faith. Try it yourself.

Take what works, leave what doesn’t. Is your day getting better? I tried it. No faith required.

You are not your character. You carry the whole world around on your head, and there is no difference between you and the world.

Refuse to suffer. Calmness is the absence of fear. Joy is proof of work.

It all comes down to this. Don’t lie. Don’t dive alone. Look for the people who’ve outgrown “I’m OK, you’re not OK.” Don’t distrust them: they wish others well too. Don’t just put up with things. Be grateful, and be glad.

Live your life as the final copy, no rough drafts: no regrets. Live for the fun of it and help others live. And see what happens.

This is what life is about. Just having a good time. At all times.

No one saves us but ourselves. We ourselves must walk the path.
Buddha
