Others

Don Miguel Ruiz fit all of human relationships into four agreements, and they work. But I came up with my own short version first.

My rules: don’t lie, don’t deal with jerks, and don’t be one yourself.

That covers most of it. The four agreements just fill in the details.

The first agreement is to be impeccable with your word. Impeccable means beyond reproach, and reproach is when you pick holes in everything. Just remember that bad hurts more than good does: a careless line gets remembered for a lifetime. Say “who even writes code like this?” in a code review, and that person will remember it forever. If you have to say something hard, add something good to it.

We talk, we don’t chatter. And when we talk straight, it’s as straight as it gets.

The second is not to take anything personally. For me it’s the most important of the four. Keep your outward self-importance to a minimum. Everybody has their reasons: they’re tired, they’re upset. Often people don’t even mean what they say. Something got triggered inside, and they just want to push back a little. Get that, and you’ll save friendships and keep things good with the people you work with. No one can offend you: the offense is something you pick up and carry yourself.

You always own the option of having no opinion. There is never any need to get worked up or to trouble your soul about things you can’t control. — Marcus Aurelius

The third is not to make assumptions. Something goes wrong? Ask. Don’t assume the other person knows what you know, or that they’ll just take care of it. If the ticket is too big, say so. That alone clears up a ton of problems.

And the fourth is to always do your best. That’s what sold me on the book: your best will be different from one day to the next, and what matters is the intent. Then there’s nothing to regret about the past either. You did your best, you thought it through. Where would regret even come from?

Don’t be the irritation

Getting along with people is largely a matter of not irritating them. Be friendly.

Trust first

My default is open. You already have my trust; distrust is what you’d have to earn.

People usually run it the other way: they guard everything, make others prove their worth, and only then, maybe, lower the shield. That’s exhausting, and it keeps the good people away too.

Drop the armor and trust first. The few who abuse it weed themselves out fast.

Help, and don’t expect anything back. A transaction is finite: deal closed.

Hire people bigger than you

Some people put others down to look bigger next to them. Nature doesn’t work that way: whatever you push away comes back at you.

I hired people smarter than me. If the team doesn’t make it, everything lands on me, so what would be the point of hiring weaker people? Hire people bigger than you, and they’ll pull you up to their level. And don’t put anyone down. Just be equals, and be happy.

The people around you shape more than your mood. Over time, the voices around you become the voices in your head — which is exactly where we’re going next.
