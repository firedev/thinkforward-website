Love

I have a model I like to share, and not everyone gets it. Drumroll: the ideal relationship is friendship. Wait, don’t laugh.

Friendship = Love. And love is a verb.

Love is active care for the one you love. It’s something you do, every day and on purpose. The best partner is your best friend, and you build that the way you build any friendship: by always being there.

The stages of love

If love is a verb, it has stages, and you can watch them unfold.

First, stop. Karma isn’t a sentence handed down from above; karma means doing. So do: stop the cycle. Stop feeling guilty, stop blaming others, stop hunting for flaws.

Stop doing what you know you shouldn’t be doing. Start stopping now.

Then heal yourself. Meditate. Look at what you feel and where the thoughts and feelings come from. Research, seek: it’s not what it seems.

Then refuse to suffer. And whatever you’ve been given, pass it on.

Couple

Even with friendship at the base, a couple needs tuning. Here’s what helps.

The best relationship is a friendship in which you wish the other person well just because, without jealousy. Falling in love is a flash of happiness, a hormonal anomaly. Love is friendship and joy, a long, slow burn.

The first thing is talking: talk, talk, and then talk some more. Almost everything that breaks between two people breaks over something neither of them said. We started by talking through everything that came before, the whole backlog, out loud.

Anya and I keep working through moments as they come up. We started by talking out everything that happened before.

The second is the love languages. People give and receive love in different currencies. Find out which one your partner actually feels, and pay in that one.

The third is staying yourself and not dissolving into the couple: the two of you stay interesting to each other as long as each stays a whole person.

And the fourth is desire. Love rests on conversation, a shared life and habit, and also on touch, on wanting to be close, on feeling wanted. When someone loves you, they want you freely, because they’re drawn to you in particular. So read desire honestly: it shows where your connection stands right now.

Sex isn’t everything, but it’s a real measure of interest, attraction, and chemistry. Love is also expressed through touch and wanting to be close.

It’s a pleasant job, and still a job. You keep showing up, keep sorting out what’s going on with you, and keep choosing each other.

It’s probably called “working on the relationship,” but I don’t think of it as work. You just have to keep figuring out what’s going on with you.

Children

You, your partner and your children: get those three right, and you have a family. That’s the part of life that lives inside you.

Family is a bike with training wheels. It’s where a person learns to balance before riding off alone.

And parenting takes less than you think. I’m for minimum parenting. Don’t wear yourself out trying to engineer the perfect child: they’ll grow up like you anyway, so the work to do is on yourself.

Don’t raise your kids — they’ll turn out like you regardless. Work on yourself. — Stoyanov

Berne wrote a whole book about it: children living out their parents’ script. And you hand that script down by example; lectures have nothing to do with it.

So set a modest goal, and the right one. You’ve done your job if your kids can support themselves, grew up decent people, and don’t blame you for everything that’s wrong in their lives.

One more thing: don’t let them make you stop liking them. Relax. You and the kids will be just fine.

The inside is in order. Time to turn outward.

Love is not a feeling you fall into — it’s a skill you practice. It’s a verb, an active concern for the life and growth of what we love, and like any art it has to be learned.

The Secret to Love That Lasts

People give and receive love in five different ways — words of affirmation, quality time, gifts, acts of service, and physical touch. Love stays alive when you learn to speak your partner’s language, not your own.
